# Prioritization & Roadmapping, Module 2 Lab

## Prioritize your Rocks and your Hard Nos
- **Rock #1:** #7, Foreman-facing mobile app. This isn't just high impact, it's the vehicle itself. Where-to-play named a lightweight, purpose-built mobile view separate from the PM web app. Without this shipping, nothing else in the strategy has anywhere to live. If only one item in this backlog ships, it has to be this one.
- **Rock #2:** #1, Offline-first mobile experience. Directly the second world-class capability we just locked. Job-site connectivity is exactly the condition your winning aspiration depends on surviving, "receive the answer the moment it exists" is a broken promise the first time a foreman loses signal at the wrong moment. High effort, but it's non-negotiable, not optional polish.
- **Rock #3:** #9, AI-assisted RFI drafting. This one earns its spot for a less obvious reason than "it mentions RFI." It's the mechanism that makes routing accuracy possible without asking the foreman to fill out a form correctly himself. If capture stays messy, your first world-class capability, correct routing without manual correction, never gets clean enough input to succeed. This is the item that makes capture "as easy as a text" while still producing structured, routable data. It's doing double duty for both the friction promise and the moat.
- **❌ Hard No #1:** #10, Executive reporting dashboard. We are not building this now, even with two renewals at risk. An executive KPI dashboard is enterprise analytics depth, the same direction the enterprise team has already pushed for, and funding it would quietly turn Foundations into an analytics initiative instead of a field-adoption one. The renewals are real, but they belong to whoever owns platform and enterprise product, not to a team whose mandate is winning the field. Protecting that boundary here is the clearest test of whether this strategy holds under real revenue pressure.
- **❌ Hard No #2:** #5, Automated compliance checklist generator. Compliance was named explicitly as one of the four functions deferred beyond RFI in this phase, and this request, sourced from enterprise sales on behalf of three clients, is that exact pull showing up in the backlog. It is a legitimate ask from real customers, which is what makes it worth declining deliberately rather than by default. Building it now would spend backend capacity we've already committed to routing accuracy and offline reliability, the two capabilities this strategy actually depends on.
- **❌ Hard No #3:** #14, Subcontractor portal with document sharing. This item carries no urgent dollar figure or renewal risk, which is exactly why it matters as a hard no. It is enterprise sales sourced, compliance and document-control adjacent, and easy to wave through precisely because nothing forces the question. Saying no here shows the guardrail against enterprise-direction drift holds even when there's no immediate cost to ignoring it, not just when the stakes are visible.
