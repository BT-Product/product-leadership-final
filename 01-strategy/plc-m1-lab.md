# Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, Module 1 Lab

## Write your one-page strategy
- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:** We win when a foreman or superintendent can send what's happening on site and get back what he needs to keep working, an answer, a status, a decision, as fast and as easily as sending a text. Project managers and finance win by inheriting a live, accurate record of the job site instead of a text thread they were never part of.
- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:** We play inside our 38 existing top-100 enterprise accounts, focused on the foreman and superintendent RFI workflow specifically, submitting and receiving RFI resolutions through a lightweight, purpose-built mobile view. Budget-variance, compliance, document control, multi-site status, and the broader top-100 market remain explicitly out of scope for this phase.
- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:** A competitor can build a clean RFI capture app. None of them can correctly route it, track it against this project's specific approval chain and compliance obligations, or notify the foreman the instant a real answer exists, because that requires already being the system those approvals and obligations live in. We win by making capture as easy as a text, and making everything after capture invisible and automatic, because we're the only ones who already know where it needs to go.
- **Capabilities required: what you must be world-class at. What will you build, buy, or partner for?:** We must be world-class at two things. First, routing accuracy, correctly mapping a captured RFI to the right approval chain for the specific project and firm, without manual correction, built in-house because it depends on account configuration depth no competitor has. Second, offline-first reliability, ensuring a submitted RFI and its returning answer survive poor or intermittent job-site connectivity through store-and-forward and retry logic, buy the underlying notification infrastructure, but the offline behavior itself must be engineered and tested specifically for construction-site conditions. Capture UX must be simple enough to clear a first-use bar, but it is not the moat.

Our current team composition, weighted toward mobile engineering, was sized for a broader mobile build than this scope requires. We reallocate within the existing 10, fewer mobile engineering seats, added backend and systems engineering depth, to match the team to routing and offline reliability, the two capabilities that actually carry this strategy.
- **Management systems: the metrics and rituals that keep your choices alive quarter to quarter.:** We track last-mile notification latency, routing accuracy, and offline sync success rate as proof the winning aspiration is real, and first-session completion as the adoption gate it depends on. Guardrails, reviewed quarterly with leadership, confirm team composition is held to its backend-and-systems reallocation, and that no deferred function, compliance, budget-variance, document control, or multi-site, is reconsidered until a defined performance threshold on routing accuracy and adoption is met. A recurring foreman panel keeps the team anchored to real field friction, and a monthly engineering review protects the two capabilities the strategy actually depends on.

## Name your one hard no
- **Your one hard no:** We will not build parity across Meridian's other enterprise functions, compliance, budget-variance, document control, multi-site coordination, in this phase, even though enterprise sales and clients with renewals at risk are actively asking for them, because collapsing our full team's focus onto a single workflow is what makes routing accuracy and offline reliability world-class rather than merely adequate. A team split five ways delivers five mediocre experiences. A team aimed at one delivers something a foreman actually trusts.

## Write your three OKRs
- **Objective:** Make Meridian the first place a foreman turns to resolve an RFI, without sacrificing the routing accuracy enterprise teams depend on.
- **KR1:** Increase the share of RFIs submitted and resolved through the field app, rather than phone call or text, from near-zero, Meridian's own data shows field teams currently don't use the platform, to 40% across RFI-enabled projects
- **KR2:** Achieve 95% or higher routing accuracy, field-submitted RFIs correctly mapped to the right approval chain with no manual PM correction, across all RFI-enabled projects.
- **KR3:** Deliver 95% of RFI resolutions to the foreman within 5 minutes of the answer becoming available, including cases where the device was offline at the moment of resolution.

## AI pressure-test
- **Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why?:** The biggest challenge is that we don't have evidence from foremen and superintendents in these 38 accounts that, if Meridian becomes as easy as texting, they will actually move their RFI behavior from calls/texts into Meridian. Up to this point we have created a bet based on data and assumptions, but we could build brilliant routing, offline sync, and notifications and discover six months later that the foreman still texts Mike because Mike answers him, he trusts Mike, and that's how work actually gets done.
- **What would you change based on the pushback, and what would you defend?:** Before the ten-person team commits to the full capability build, routing accuracy, offline reliability, the reallocation itself, there should be a small, cheap test first: put a rough version of RFI submission and notification in front of a handful of actual foremen at one or two accounts, and watch what they do, not what they say. If the friction hypothesis is right, we'll see behavior shift even with a rough build. If it's wrong, we'll see them route around it the same way they route around Meridian today.

AI mentioned RFI-only scope shouldn't be a hard no because a RFI is not necessarily a self-contained transaction. It suggested to "remove the categorical exclusion, allow a thin slice whenever it improves the RFI workflow," However, this is dangerous. "Whenever it directly improves the workflow" is exactly the kind of soft, well-intentioned language that lets scope creep back in gradually.
